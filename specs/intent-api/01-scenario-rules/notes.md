## Results and limits (2026-11-01)
Full numbers and every test run are in `apps/intent-api/docs/laya/09-intent-api-notes.md`.

- Pipeline: Laya (`typed-decisions`, own process) chooses the domain (six domains and `Other`, short descriptions, the question passed as `{"question": text}`), then the scenarios inside it (the question is split at "and", commas and "then", one choice per clause with a "nothing is asked" option); rules own the library, `covers`, the floors and every refusal. 14 scenarios (Sales, Purchase); Finance, Shipments, Inventory and Production have a description but no scenarios yet and are refused with the domain named.
- Floors chosen with the owner: `DOMAIN_MIN_PROBABILITY` 0, `SCENARIO_MIN_PROBABILITY` 0.3. Held-out questions (34, run once): 19 of 24 answerable exactly right (79%), 9 of 10 must-refuse refused, domain right 33 of 34, 5 of 34 (15%) wrong answers where a refusal or another scenario was right. Tuning questions at the same floors: 36 of 50 exact, 11 of 79 wrong answers.
- Compared with the first Laya test (one yes/no per scenario, 16 scenarios, 34 answerable questions): exactly right on 3 questions at 0.7 and 6 at 0.8. The same yes/no design measured again here gave 2 and 9 of 33. Short descriptions, clauses and one choice per clause give 27 of 33 on the same reference questions, and 79% on the held-out set.
- What did not work: long keyword lists as descriptions (46 against 52 of 55 once shortened), one yes/no per domain or scenario, two layers of domain choice, a separate in-scope yes/no gate, a broad `none` option. What did: short descriptions, a named field in the state, `Other: everything else`, clauses.
- Limits: the questions were written by us (the numbers are for friendly phrasing); comparison wording ("up or down against", "relative to") is read as value, trend or compare-with-last-year; forecasts and margins that look like sales questions can be answered when the probability is high; a misspelling can still flip the domain with high confidence ("by in septembr" read as Sales at 0.82 to 1.00); the held-out set is spent for threshold choices; one worker, one call at a time (about 1.3 s per question, more for multi-clause questions); no mixed-domain questions; no slots (periods, numbers, names) yet.
